Abstract

Our project deals with causal analysis processes and specializes in specific domain knowledge, in our case: the financial industry. Financial time series data is characterized by high complexity, dimensionality, and nonstationarity. Traditional correlation-based models in causal analysis often fail to identify true causal drivers versus spurious associations, leading to overfitting and poor generalization.

We implement a domain-specific causal discovery pipeline utilizing the PCMCI algorithm to identify causal links in financial industry data. By comparing a "naive baseline" causal discovery approach to a "domain-specialized" approach, we demonstrate improved performance on real financial industry datasets. Our specialized pipeline incorporates optimization for time lags, significance levels, and constraints for stationarity. Our results show a reduction in spurious predictors and significant improvement in predictive error on unseen test data.

1. Introduction

The main issue with causal relation analysis is the identification of spurious correlations, especially in the context of the financial domain. In a macroeconomic environment, asset prices and rates often move together not as a result of causal relationships, but rather because they have common underlying confounders.

Current standard feature selection methodologies often fail to identify true causal relationships from spurious correlations. This in turn creates predictive models that perform well on the training data but fail on unseen data. Our project proposes a pipeline that utilizes Tigramite's PCMCI algorithm—a causal discovery algorithm that works well for time series data—to create causal graphs. Our main goal is to demonstrate that by filtering out "instantaneous" noise and using "lagged" causality, we are able to build a linear regression model that is simpler, more interpretable, and performs better than a baseline model.

2. Methods

2.1 Acquiring Financial Datasets

To acquire our financial domain datasets, we utilized two openly available APIs to scrape data dating from 2000 to 2025:

  • Federal Reserve Economic Data (FRED): Unemployment rate, inflation, policy interest rate, GDP, yield curve spread, TED spread, market volatility index (VIX), and credit spread.
  • Yahoo Finance: Crude oil futures (CL=F), gold futures (GC=F), S&P 500 index (^GSPC), energy index (XLE), financials (XLF), technology (XLK), and 10-year Treasury yields (^TNX).

2.2 Data Preparation & Stationarity

Initial attempts at causal relation analysis using raw financial data resulted in a heavily saturated graph where nearly every variable seemed causally linked. This was due to the raw data violating the Stationarity Assumption required by the causal analysis independence test. To resolve this, we transformed the data: taking the log of returns for asset prices to prevent heteroscedasticity, and taking simple differences for rates and spreads.

2.3 Causal Discovery (PCMCI)

We compared two variations of the PCMCI algorithm using the ParCorr conditional independence test:

  • Baseline Model: Default hyperparameter settings (Tau=5, Alpha=0.05).
  • Specialized Model: Domain-optimized settings (Tau=2, Alpha=0.01) designed to reduce false positives and capture specific lagged causalities without introducing noise.

4. Results & Discussion

Running the baseline PCMCI algorithm (Alpha=0.05, Lag=5) resulted in an excessively dense causal graph, picking up general market growth rather than distinct dependencies. By heavily tightening our significance threshold (Alpha=0.01) and restricting the maximum lag based on domain knowledge (Tau=2), the Domain-Specialized PCMCI achieved a significant reduction in spurious predictors.

This specialization allowed us to identify fundamental drivers—such as crude oil futures directly driving the Energy Select Sector (XLE) and Fed Funds rates impacting the 10-year treasury yield—while avoiding the overfitting trap seen in the naive baseline approach.

5. Conclusion

We summarize our key findings by emphasizing the importance of careful hyperparameter selection and domain knowledge in financial causal discovery. The optimized pipeline produces dynamic causal graphs (DCGs) that are far more interpretable for macroeconomic environments and result in out-of-sample predictive models that generalize reliably.
