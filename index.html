Introduction

Financial markets are full of patterns. But when variables move together, it does not always mean one causes the other.

Stock prices, interest rates, and macroeconomic indicators often shift at the same time, sometimes because they are influenced by broader economic forces. These misleading correlations can make a model look strong on training data but unreliable in real-world use.

In this project, we use PCMCI to uncover meaningful cause-and-effect relationships in financial data. Our goal is to build forecasting models that are both more interpretable and more robust.

Data

This study uses a wide range of financial and macroeconomic data from 2000 onward. We collected data from the following three categories.

1. Macroeconomic Indicators (FRED)

  • Unemployment rate
  • Inflation
  • Policy interest rate
  • GDP
  • Yield curve spread
  • TED spread
  • Market volatility index (VIX)
  • Credit spread

2. Financial Assets and Sector Data (Yahoo Finance)

  • Crude oil and gold futures
  • S&P 500 index
  • Energy, financial, and technology sector ETFs
  • 10-year Treasury yield

3. Exchange Rate Data (Alpha Vantage)

  • USD/EUR
  • USD/KRW
  • USD/CAD

All data were aligned and merged on a daily basis. For time-series causal analysis, price data were transformed into log returns, while rate and indicator data were transformed using first differences.

Through this process, we constructed an integrated time-series network representing interactions across the financial system.
